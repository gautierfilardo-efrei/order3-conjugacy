Anticipated referee objections and prepared answers

1. "The headline problem (P3) is left open."  -- Yes, stated in the abstract and Section 6. The paper classifies the
   restricted problem for every order n, proves that no word/orbit/group invariant decides P3 (Gassmann pair), reduces P3
   to an exact question (one equation vs. the two-equation system) and proves that the natural paddings fail. These are the
   results; the open problem is located, not hidden.
2. "Is the Conj_n theorem new?"  -- Literature search (Crossref/arXiv, 2026-10-07; novelty_report.md): nothing on conjugators of
   prescribed order. Closest: Mattes-Ushakov-Weiss (LATIN 2024) on spherical equations over S_n with n in the input;
   Lohrey-Rosowski-Zetzsche (J. Algebra 2025) on membership in products of cyclic groups. Both have groups/cosets given by
   generators or as products; ours is an implicit coset of a centraliser.
3. "Conj_2 polynomial is trivial / known."  -- Only the case c = a^{-1} (strongly real elements) is classical; the general
   problem with two arbitrary constants is not in the literature; the algorithm (orbit pairing with a parity condition) is
   short but not immediate. Verified on 409,113 pairs with N <= 9 and by two independent scripts.
4. "The PSL(2,7) example is just a Gassmann triple."  -- Acknowledged and cited (Perlis, Sunada, Kammeyer-Kionke). The novelty
   is its use as an obstruction for equation solving, and the exhaustive solution counts (0 vs 1).
5. "The first-moment identity is routine."  -- Said so in the text; it is included because it is used (fourlayer remark,
   statistics) and we found no statement of it in the literature.
6. "Section 5 is empirical and about an unpublished construction."  -- It is an application section; the theorem there
   (wreath normal form) is exact, the cost law is reported as an interval over two models and two branching rules, and all
   figures are regenerated from recorded data. We are willing to shorten it on request.
7. "Verification by scripts is not proof."  -- Every theorem has a written proof; the scripts are exhaustive checks on small
   degrees, cited with their instance counts, and are archived (release v0.4.0). Two lemmas that were sketches in an earlier
   draft now have complete proofs (cube roots with prescribed images; cube roots in a centraliser).
8. "Reduction exponential in n."  -- Polynomial for each fixed n, which is what the classification needs; the uniform question
   is listed as open problem 5.
