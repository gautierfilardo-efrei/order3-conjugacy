Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "One-variable equations of length three over symmetric groups: normal forms, the
hardness of conjugacy by an element of prescribed order, and layered permutation walks", for publication in the Journal of
Symbolic Computation.

The paper studies the equation x a x^2 = c over Sym(N) with N part of the input. Every one-variable word with at most two
occurrences of the unknown is decided in polynomial time from cycle types, and every positive word of length three is
equivalent to this one, so it is a shortest one-variable shape over permutation groups whose complexity is not classified.

Main results.
(1) Deciding whether two permutations are conjugate by an element of order n is NP-complete for every n >= 3 (reduction from
    numerical n-dimensional matching through a blockwise family in which all conjugators are affine) and polynomial, in O(N^3)
    time, for n = 2 (pairing the orbits of the group generated by the two constants). The classification is complete.
    Earlier hardness results of this kind (Lubiw; Cameron and Wu; Arvind; Lohrey and Rosowski) concern groups or cosets given
    by generators; here the coset is implicit, defined by two permutations only.
(2) A pair of permutations in Sym(7) generating PSL(2,7) -- the classical Gassmann pair of degree seven -- for which every word
    in the two constants has the same cycle type on both sides while one equation is solvable and the other is not; no
    invariant built from cycle types of words, orbit structure or group data can decide solvability.
(3) An exact closed form for the first moment of the number of solutions, polynomial special cases with complete proofs
    (bounded support of a, solutions commuting with a, pairs of involutions), and the identity "conjugacy by an element of
    order three = the system of the two equations x a x^2 = c, x a^2 x^2 = c^2", together with a proof that the natural
    paddings of this system into a single equation acquire spurious solutions.
(4) As an application, the round of a two-register layered permutation walk recently proposed as a one-way function is
    exactly this equation in the wreath product Alt(m) wr C_2, and we measure the cost of constraint-propagation solvers.

We state explicitly what remains open: the complexity of the single equation x a x^2 = c. The paper locates the difficulty
precisely -- one equation against two -- and bounds it on both sides.

All exhaustive and randomised verifications are archived with their outputs in a public repository, and every theorem added
in the final revision was re-verified by an independent script written from the statements alone.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. We suggest as potential reviewers researchers in computational group
theory and the complexity of equations over finite groups (e.g. the authors of the LATIN 2024 paper on spherical equations
over symmetric groups, or of the recent work on cycle-type and membership problems in permutation groups).
